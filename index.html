how to use

1 · load a picture
Open a photo of a QR code, drag one in, or paste it. Your work is saved in the browser as you go, so reloading the page picks up where you left off. The save button downloads everything — picture and work — as one file you can reopen later or send to someone.

2 · line up the grid
A grid is laid over the code with a dot on every square, showing black or white as the app reads it. It usually finds its place by itself; when it doesn't, drag the blue diamond handles: the corner ones move the corners, the middle ones bend the grid for codes on curved surfaces, the circle above spins it, the arrows outside the corners stretch it, and the cross below slides the whole thing. Dragging a corner arrow across to the far side flips the grid, for mirror-image codes. Arrow keys nudge the selected handle (hold shift for tiny steps). If the code has more or fewer squares than the grid, change "version" — that's a QR code's size.

3 · trust the red marks
Parts of every QR code are always the same, so the app knows what those squares should be — they get small square marks instead of round dots. They turn red where the picture disagrees: one or two may be real damage, but many red dots mean the grid is sitting wrong — adjust until they go away. The threshold slider sets how dark counts as black, and the quiet zone option also checks the blank border every code must have around it.

4 · read the result
The decoded text appears below the picture; red underlined letters are unreliable. Under "codewords", the raw bytes are grouped into blocks (B1, B2, …): ✓ means fully readable, +n means n mistakes were repaired, e counts squares you marked damaged, ✕ means too damaged. Point at any letter, byte, or block to light up the squares it came from — and point at a square on the picture to see where it lands in the text.

5 · fix squares by hand
Only the round dots can be changed — the square marks are fixed by the QR standard and carry no data. Click a round dot to overrule what the app read: force it white, force it black, or have it ignored (right-click puts it back to automatic). Hold shift and drag a box over a hopeless area — a sticker, a hole, a logo — to ignore all of it. Ignoring genuinely helps: when the app knows where the damage is, it can repair twice as much of it. Every change is undoable (ctrl/⌘Z), and the result updates as you work.
